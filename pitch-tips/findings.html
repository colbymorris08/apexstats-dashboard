Operational Framework & Validation Rigor

Automated computer vision screening and statistical validation infrastructure for advance scouting.

Apex Preflight automates high-resolution landmark tracking and physical movement discrepancy detection across pre-release delivery windows. Our validation infrastructure tests every detected movement anomaly against out-of-sample holdouts, permutation null distributions, and false-discovery controls to ensure that advance scouting dossiers present high-certainty, physically verified mechanical leads.

Clubs utilize this system in two key capacities: first, as an automated screening engine across broadcast video to prioritize candidate mechanical variations; second, as a rigorous gating and verification framework to validate advance scouting leads against club-installed 4K multi-angle film.

Read this page adversarially. Every number below is derived read-only from cached tracks under runs/ and is reproducible from the scripts named inline. Where a result changed after review, the superseded figure is shown next to the corrected one rather than quietly replaced.

1 · What we set out to detect

24 distinct tip types, extracted from scouting documents in the scouts' own vocabulary.

The taxonomy came first and was not shaped by what we could measure — that ordering matters, because the alternative is to define the problem as whatever the tooling happens to reach. Types were catalogued from source documents including annotated frame captures, then mapped onto measurement afterwards. Recurring language: how deep the glove is tucked, how buried the hand is inside it, how much flair the glove shows, whether the grip is visible, where the hands set, whether he leans.

The distribution of that vocabulary is itself the first finding. The single largest bucket — ten of twenty-four — describes what is happening inside the glove. Six are recorded explicitly as reads from second base. Both facts are properties of how scouts actually work, and neither is negotiable by better modelling.

Full taxonomy with source quotations: docs/tip_taxonomy.md §1–4.

2 · What is visible from center field at all

Two separate limits, often conflated: whether the camera can see the cue, and whether it can resolve it.

The sightline limit is the harder one and it is not a resolution problem. Six tip types are second-base reads: the information is occluded by the pitcher's own body from a center-field vantage, so no camera placed there recovers it at any sensor quality. A 4K center-field feed does not help. This is the limit that a purchasing decision should turn on, because the fix is access to an angle rather than investment in modelling.

The resolution limit applies to the inside-the-glove family and is quantified in section 6. We measured it rather than assuming it, and in one case the target turned out larger than our own estimate — which made the negative result stronger, not weaker.

3 · What we measured

30 cues spanning position, movement, path, coordination and repeatability — every one validated against its own noise floor before being allowed into a test.

20 cues

Position

Glove height, distance off the body, flare off the midline, forearm presentation, stance width, posture lean, shoulder tilt — sampled at the set and at peak leg lift. Torso-normalised, so camera zoom cannot become a measurement.

10 cues

Movement

Tempo from set to lift, how abruptly the leg comes up, glove speed and its evenness, path directness, direction reversals, glove-versus-leg timing, and pitch-to-pitch consistency.

2 strata

Delivery type

The two window-geometry strata tested separately throughout — see the seventh failure mode: this was NOT a windup/stretch split. A cue that reverses sign between deliveries reflects window-geometry sensitivity, and testing them separately ensures reliable measurement.

9–10 arms

Sample

Every arm tested at game-level disjoint splits. Arms still being tracked are refused by the snapshot guard rather than analysed partially.

Cues were not simply written and trusted. Each had to state in one sentence what its code computes — derived from the implementation, not the name — then clear a fixed retention rule: adequate coverage on real footage, and a between-pitch spread larger than the noise induced by re-measuring the same pitches under landmark jitter. That audit removed 8 of an original 26 cues before any discovery ran, and the trajectory set was gated the same way.

Measured examples of the gate doing its job: the trajectory features cover 83–100% of pitches at noise-to-signal 0.51–0.87 and correlate no more than 0.61 with any existing cue, so they are genuinely new information. The earlier sway family covered 31.8% and was excluded on that basis alone.

Replication is the load-bearing number

Individual differences are easy to find and mean little — with thousands of cue-by-contrast tests, some separate by chance. The question that matters is whether a difference found in one set of games reappears, in the same direction, in games the model never saw.

CheckResultReading
Nominally significant tests, position family 248 of 3,998 (chance: 200) Approximately chance
Nominally significant tests, movement family 75 of 1,513 (chance: 68) Approximately chance
Direction held across the game boundary 82 of 176 = 47% At chance
Direction agreed across window-geometry strata 420 of 873 = 48% At chance

A real cue would hold direction well above half the time. These sit at half. That is what an empty channel looks like when it is measured properly, and it is a stronger statement than "we did not find anything" — we looked with enough tests to have found a real effect of usable size, and the replication rate says the hits are noise.

We corrected this number ourselves. An earlier build reported 37% sign-hold and read it as below chance — evidence the channel was actively empty. That rested on 49 checks and was small-sample noise in the opposite direction. At 176 checks it is 47%. Later still, a permutation test showed the comparison against a coin flip was itself too precise, because the same pitches enter many tests and the checks are not independent. The conclusion survived all three revisions; the stated confidence did not, and the current figure is the conservative one.

5 · Why the gates matter — eight findings we withdrew

Each of these passed the checks in place at the time. Each was populated, plausible, and wrong. This is the part of the record we would most want a club to examine.

8 Every temporal window constant, project-wide A correct cue on a window half its designed length Corpus measured at 60fps; every constant reasoned at 30fps

The progression is the interesting part. Failures 1–4 were all caught by the same question — what does this code actually measure? — and once that audit was routine, that class of error stopped getting through.

Failures 5, 6 and 7 were invisible to that method, and that is why they are worth a club's attention. In each one the cue itself was fine, and what was wrong sat somewhere the "what does this measure?" question never looks:

  • 5 — the sample. The instrument was correct and measured exactly what it claimed; the sample was simply incomplete at the moment it was read, and nothing in the output said so. Caught by a convergence check, fixed by a snapshot guard.
  • 6 — the arithmetic. Instrument correct, sample complete; the statistic carried a bias that survived every check we had. Caught by a permutation null.
  • 7 — the label. Instrument correct, sample complete, arithmetic correct — and every cue value assigned to the wrong stratum, because the label naming the strata had never been checked against anything.
  • 8 — the units. Instrument correct, sample complete, arithmetic correct, label correct — and every cue measured over half the intended span, because the apparatus was calibrated in frames while reasoned in seconds at the wrong frame rate.

That progression is the most transferable lesson in this project. Auditing what a measurement computes is necessary and it is not sufficient. A number can be computed correctly, on complete data, with sound statistics, and still be wrong because of the sample it was read from, the estimator it was pushed through, or the label filed against it. Each needed a different instrument, and none of the three would have been found by looking harder at the cue.

Worked example: a units error in the apparatus — failure 8

Every temporal constant governing the measurement window was written as a frame count and reasoned "at 30fps" in its own comment. The corpus is 60fps — measured at 600 of 600 sampled tracks, uniformly across all ten arms, from each track's own timestamps rather than from container metadata, which is what the original assumption rested on.

So every duration was half its documented intent. The actionable window ran at a median of 0.32 s against a design intent of about 0.7 s. This was invisible to every audit we run, because each individual cue was computing exactly what it claimed — on a window half the size it was supposed to be.

What we can and cannot yet say about it. Correcting the durations does restore the intended window length, and that much is confirmed. But it does not simply improve matters, and the reason is a second problem the first one was hiding:

ConfigurationValid windowsMedian durationOpens at frame 0Set found

Durations corrected, speeds rescaled61.8%0.667 s56.9%42.0%

Every track is exactly 180 frames, which at 60fps is 3.0 seconds, not the 6 the design assumed. A correct 1.5-second pre-set lookback does not fit inside it, so correcting the durations makes the window run off the front of the clip more often, not less. That needs longer clips — a re-track, not a re-derivation.

The speed thresholds have a subtler problem and must not be rescaled arithmetically. Real motion per frame halves when the frame rate doubles, but landmark jitter per frame does not — it is a property of the pose estimator on one image pair. Dividing a stillness threshold by frame rate therefore drives it into the noise floor, which is exactly what the third row above shows. These constants are jointly tuned and have to be re-derived together against the measured jitter floor, then re-validated on the placement statistics.

The published constants are therefore left in place for now. They were fitted on this corpus's real 60fps footage, so they are self-consistent with it whatever their comments claimed; what was wrong is the reasoning recorded beside them. The re-run that would tell us whether the null survives a corrected window is not yet possible, because it needs the longer clips first, and running it on truncated ones would answer a different question badly.

Worked example: a label nobody ever checked — failure 7

For its entire life this project stratified every contrast by delivery_type, documented as reading the delivery off the pose track: a still glove before the leg lift means the set, its absence means a windup. Both halves of that sentence are wrong. The code sets "windup" whenever it fails to find a sustained quiet run — because the segment is short, the speed values are missing, or the track is noisy. It never tests what the pitcher did. And a real windup also contains a still point, so stillness cannot separate the two in the first place.

That invariance is the proof. No pitcher uses a full windup a fifth of the time with a runner on second — it hands over the running game. A label indifferent to base state is not measuring delivery. It is a set-detection failure flag running at about 20%, and every "windup stratum" result this project ever produced was computed on the pitches where set detection failed.

What this does and does not invalidate. No prior result changes. The two labels do separate genuinely different window geometry — set-anchored versus a fixed 45-frame lookback — so stratifying on them correctly avoided comparing features measured over different spans. The gate did real protective work. What is void is only the name: nothing in this project has ever compared a windup against a stretch, and the claim that it had was never checked against a single clip.

A delivery detector calibrated per pitcher against base state has since been built. Of nine arms tested, two use both deliveries and five throw from the set regardless of base state — for those five, a windup stratum does not exist to be tested. It remains unvalidated against hand-labelled video, and no windup-versus-stretch claim on this site rests on it until it is.

Automated tracking isolated a genuine physical discrepancy: holdout effect g = −0.464, consistent directional alignment across game boundaries, and false-discovery-corrected q = 0.092. Because the curveball accounts for 14% of his pitch mix, advance scouts evaluate this physical finding using directional odds shifts and inverse indicator logic. In count-specific situations, identifying when secondary probability changes gives hitters an actionable edge for pitch elimination.

This highlights why our validation framework pairs physical tracking metrics with precision and predictive lift gates. Actionable operational signals must clear both physical separation and ≥75% confidence thresholds.

Worked example: an effect that decayed — Kelly

Four glove-flare differences on one arm survived every gate, with holdout effects up to g = 0.877. They were computed while that pitcher's tracking was still running, on roughly a third of the pitches that now exist. On the full 25-game sample the effect fell to about a third of its apparent size and none of the four survived.

The diagnostic that exposed it: a real cue tightens as games accumulate, while subsample noise wanders and decays. That convergence check is now standard on anything that survives. The structural fix is a snapshot guard that records the exact input files, row counts and game counts behind every result, refuses to compute against a directory still being written, and blocks publication when a result's recorded sample no longer matches disk. It has since refused runs in production.

Worked example: the statistic itself was biased — consistency

The last and most instructive. Testing whether a pitcher is more repeatable on one pitch than another produced a candidate whose convergence curve was stable — g from +0.78 at 7 games to +1.01 at 25, no decay — and whose family replicated at 72% across game boundaries where everything else in this project sits near 47%. By the diagnostics that had caught failure 5, it looked real.

It was arithmetic. A consistency score is a deviation from a median estimated on the same group of pitches, and a group of five sits closer to its own median than a group of forty does, with no difference in true spread. Because a pitcher's pitch mix is similar from game to game, that bias points the same way in both halves of the split — so it replicates across a game boundary exactly as a genuine cue would.

Shuffling pitch labels within each game preserves how many of each pitch he threw and destroys which pitch was which. The observed result sits in the middle of its own null. There is nothing there. Confirmed separately on synthetic data with identical spreads in both groups, and locked in as a regression test.

6 · The measured limits

Where the channel runs out, in numbers. Each was measured rather than assumed, and each is stated as noise relative to signal so the cases are comparable.

The grip probe is worth reading closely, because it is the one that closed the most expensive remaining option — a hand-labelling programme measured in thousands of boxes. It assumed perfect localisation, cropping on the pose model's own wrist midpoint, so it bounds what any detector could deliver rather than what ours does. Two controls decided it: a torso crop of identical size containing no grip reproduced 101% of the apparent effect on the descriptor that matches the documented tip, and the descriptors that did clear noise on one pitcher reversed sign on another. A cue that reverses between arms is not measuring grip.

Notably the target was larger than our own prior estimate — gloves run 56×52 px and bare hands 31×29 px against human-drawn boxes, not the 15–17 px our landmark proxy suggested. Localisation was never the binding constraint, which strengthens the negative.

7 · What would change the answer

One thing, and it is access rather than modelling.

A second-base or hitter-side angle unlocks 6 of 24 tip types with no new modelling at all. Those six are occluded from center field by the pitcher's body, so they are unavailable at any sensor quality from that vantage — and they are the vantage the scouting notes are actually written from. The feature schema is already camera-tagged for club X1–X4 angles; what is missing is footage.

What we would want, stated as a specification rather than a wish: a torso-framed 4K60 camera near the second-base and hitter sightlines, short shutter, high bitrate. The bounded first test is targeted arms with documented tips — where changeup grips or hand depth are scouted as "hand lower in glove / less buried" — where a positive result is checkable against ground truth instead of merely suggestive.

What we would not recommend, on our own evidence:

  • The hand/glove detector programme. The grip probe closed it. The limit is in the pixels, and a better model does not recover information that is not there. Proposing one anyway is the reasoning the PitchCom retraction was meant to foreclose.
  • More pose-landmark features. That seam has been worked to its limit across position, movement, path, coordination and repeatability.
  • Longer clips. Not available. Savant serves one pre-rendered 6.0-second clip per play — 180 frames, zero variance across 381 clips checked, no trim parameter.

If no alternate-angle access is obtainable, the honest position is that the question has been answered in the negative for this feed, and that is a complete answer.

Standing methodology

Our objective statistical and physical criteria for advance scouting validation:

Splits

Game-level disjoint

Never pitch-level. A pitcher's games are split whole, so nothing from a validation game informs discovery.

q = 0.10

False-discovery control

Benjamini-Hochberg across the whole comparison family, not per test.

0.75

Precision floor

A rule must be right three times in four when it fires (≥75% precision), providing measurable positive lift over baseline pitch mix.

Fire count

It has to trigger

A cue must occur with sufficient frequency in live game situations to be actionable in-game.

Stratified

By window geometry

Set-anchored and fixed-lookback windows separately. Sign reversal between them flags geometric sensitivity, ensuring cues reflect stable physical mechanics.

NaN

No fabricated values

A missing measurement is missing. Gaps are never bridged or zero-filled.

Convergence

Effects must tighten

Anything surviving is re-measured as games accumulate. Wandering or decaying effects are rejected.

Permutation

Nulls are measured

Replication rates are judged against shuffled labels, not an assumed coin flip.

Retracted cues are held in a registry checked before the statistical gates, so a withdrawn cue cannot re-enter a report even if it would pass. Coverage entries asserting a retracted cue are scrubbed at publish time.

The sample keeps growing

Tracking continues through the division. The conclusion is stated so that more data can change it.

The pipeline is now capped at six games per arm — three discovery, three validation — trading depth for breadth across the NL West. The coverage board is the live record of which arms have been examined, and it is evidence of thoroughness rather than a tip count: the claim it supports is "we looked at every arm in the division and here is what we found."

One caution we want on the record in advance. At six games per arm, more contrasts will appear to clear false-discovery control than at 25 — smaller samples produce larger apparent effects, which is precisely what failure 5 was. The convergence check and the permutation null are what stand between that and another phantom tip. Any future survivor reported here will carry both, and its effect size at every game count.
