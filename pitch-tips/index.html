Computer Vision "Spot the Difference" Engine for Pitch Tipping & Movement Variation.

Preflight is an operational computer vision proof-of-concept (PoC) that automates high-resolution landmark and bounding-box tracking to detect pitch-to-pitch mechanical variation—glove set height, forearm exposure, torso lean, hand depth, and delivery timing—strictly before ball release. Built for MLB advance scouting, the system isolates high-movement anomalies (≥75% empirical variation) from broadcast video and scales into an actionable, high-certainty enterprise platform with club-provided 4K multi-angle feeds.

Hardware & Video Physics

Broadcast CF Limits vs. Enterprise Expansion

Why standard broadcast feeds are bounded by physical optics and timing, and how private club video unlocks complete mechanical resolution.

PHYSICAL LIMIT #1

Clips Start Too Late

Standard Savant video cuts in after the pitcher is already set (median lead-in is only ~25 frames). This structurally cuts off the entire coming-set settle, pre-set glove positioning, and initial PitchCom entry.

PHYSICAL LIMIT #2

Pixel Density Ceiling

From 350–400 ft away, the pitcher’s glove spans only ~35–45 pixels and the bare hand ~30 px. Individual fingers (6 px) and 1–2 px gaps are quantized away by 8×8 H.264 compression blocks. Grip seams and micro PitchCom wrist taps are physically unresolvable.

PHYSICAL LIMIT #3

Angle & Sightline Blindspots

CF cameras look straight down the pitcher's back. Key tipping cues—facial expressions, mouth movements, and subtle glove-internal tilts—are visible only from the 3rd Base coach or 1st Base coach sightlines, projecting along the CF axis to almost zero.
